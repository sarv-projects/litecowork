## Contract and behavior

The upload collector and chunk writer share a per-Workspace/digest operation lock across
reservation, encrypted BlobStore readback, receipt commit, physical deletion, and durable
GC-fence release. The persisted `DELETING` fence remains retryable after restart, including
when the physical object was removed before a crash. Accepted references keep a shared
digest alive. This preserves the upload receipt and blob-GC recovery contract in
[STORAGE](../../docs/STORAGE.md) and the E02-S03 negative cases; no migration or API/event
shape changed in this slice.

WorkspaceRoot Resume follows [F104](../../docs/FLOWS.md),
[WORLD-RESOURCES](../../docs/WORLD-RESOURCES.md), and the route contract in
[operator-api.openapi.yaml](../../docs/schemas/operator-api.openapi.yaml). A fresh request
reopens the saved directory without following symlinks, compares its live raw and keyed
identity with the authorized Resource and private bindings, and keeps the checked handle
open through the atomic SQLite commit. The commit refreshes current-incarnation bindings,
sets the location AVAILABLE, advances PAUSED→ACTIVE, and records snapshots, events, and
the owner receipt. Exact replay resolves the receipt before opening the path. A changed
directory after startup revalidation fails closed, removes that incarnation's invalid
binding pair, and records location UNAVAILABLE while preserving PAUSED intent. Restoring
the authorized folder is insufficient until another Runtime revalidation restores
availability; Resume then remains a separate owner action. Stale versions reject without
creating a receipt. Storage failure at receipt insertion rolls back root/location and
binding-observation projections, events (including their aggregate snapshot references),
and the owner receipt.

The tests use deterministic test-only identity pseudonyms and BlobStore keys. Directory
opening, no-follow traversal, file identity comparison, Runtime readiness checks, SQLite,
event and receipt writes are real. The test fixture does not exercise the production OS
credential store or authenticated desktop IPC.
